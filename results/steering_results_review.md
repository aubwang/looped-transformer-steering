## Findings, by severity

1. **High — Section 1, lines 90–95: causality does not imply an autonomous last-position map.** Earlier positions can evolve independently of the last position and still drive it. With earlier states y_t, the actual recurrence is x_(t+1)=F(x_t;y_t), generally time-dependent. A fixed prompt alone does not freeze y_t. Replace the explanation with:

   > Earlier positions cannot depend on the last position, but the last position can depend on them. Write its update as x_(t+1)=F(x_t;y_t), where y_t collects the earlier states. For the local analysis we hold these states fixed at their settled values y*, write F(x)=F(x;y*), and idealise the selected base state as an exact fixed point F(x*)=x*. The measured forced runs allow the earlier positions to continue evolving; agreement with this frozen-state approximation is an empirical result.

   In Limits (411–413), append: “The small unpushed drift does not bound the error introduced by freezing the earlier positions.” Avoid a universal settling claim: WRITEUP.md explicitly reports period-2 prompts outside the selected steering setting.

2. **High — Section 1, lines 109–117: Proposition 1 needs regularity and correctly propagated remainders.** Differentiability alone gives o(||P||), not necessarily O(||P||²). The current proof drops a remainder and then writes exact equalities for nonlinear iterates. The result is correct for fixed finite T with suitable smoothness; the matrix-series limit is also correct, but is not a license to interchange the small-P and infinite-time limits. Replace statement/proof with:

   > Suppose F and z are twice continuously differentiable near x*, with F(x*)=x*. For each fixed integer T≥1, as P→0, δ_T=A_T P+O_T(||P||²) and z(x_T)−z(x*)=G A_T P+O_T(||P||²), where A_T=Σ_(s=1)^T J^s. The constants may depend on T. If every eigenvalue of J has modulus less than one, A_T→(I−J)^(-1)J.
   > Proof. Inductively δ_t=O(||P||) for each fixed t. Taylor expansion gives δ_(t+1)=Jδ_t+JP+O(||P||²). Substituting δ_t=A_tP+O(||P||²), with A_0=0, gives A_(t+1)=JA_t+J and hence the stated sum. Taylor expansion of z gives the readout formula. For the matrix limit, (I−J)A_T=J−J^(T+1); the eigenvalue condition implies J^(T+1)→0 and invertibility of I−J. This limit concerns the linear-response matrices; no uniform nonlinear remainder in T is asserted.
